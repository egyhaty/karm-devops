Before you start

This practical track is for engineers building cloud and DevOps skills. You should be comfortable with a Linux terminal, basic TCP/IP and Git. Each module lists its own prerequisites; no particular certification is required. Work through the labs in order, or use an individual module as a reference.

How to work through the track

Follow the ordered modules below. Each includes objectives, prerequisites, English technical explanations, Arabic takeaways, labs, validation, cleanup, troubleshooting, senior interview answers and a short interactive quiz. Mark completion after collecting evidence. Progress is saved locally and synchronizes through the existing account sign-in; the original platform tour and its progress remain available.

Use a dedicated sandbox account/subscription. Read commands before running them, set a budget alert and destroy deadline, and never use this repository's production infrastructure or database. Local validation is separate from paid cloud deployment. Quizzes provide feedback; completion is an honest self-assessment rather than a certification.

Track outcomes

Draw and diagnose a two-AZ AWS network; defend least-privilege federation and runtime secrets; operate ALB/ASG and recovery; review remote-state drift; and deploy/tear down the WordPress capstone. Collect a resource plan, packet/identity diagram, incident timeline, restore runbook and cost estimate.

The capstone requires an issued ACM certificate, matching DNS hostname and a reviewed WordPress release/hash. It creates paid infrastructure only when you explicitly apply it. Capstone lab files.

Practice, explain and adapt

Use a five-minute architecture explanation and a ten-minute incident walkthrough. Distinguish what you built, what you measured and what you would change. For any real deployment, check its data-residency requirements, regional service/SKU availability, latency and disaster-recovery region options. Do not assume all customers must use a particular region or that a demo proves regulatory compliance. Recheck current service availability/pricing before every paid lab.

Three-week AWS-first study plan · All reference lessons

  1. 1. AWS networking: trace the packet, prove the route100 min · Not completed

    Build a private network boundary, explain NAT versus IGW, and diagnose SG, NACL and return-path failures.

  2. 2. AWS identity: roles, STS and secret boundaries85 min · Not completed

    Separate trust from permissions, evaluate least privilege, and retrieve secrets through a workload role rather than embedded credentials.

  3. 3. AWS compute: ALB health, launch templates and recovery95 min · Not completed

    Operate EC2 behind an ALB, distinguish probe success from readiness, and diagnose Auto Scaling replacement loops.

  4. A good point to pause

    You have worked through several boundaries. Take a short break, then explain one of them without your notes. Return to any check you cannot yet reproduce.

  5. 4. AWS data: RDS, S3, recovery and cost tradeoffs95 min · Not completed

    Distinguish availability from recovery, test versioned object restoration, and design measurable RPO/RTO for WordPress.

  6. 5. AWS Terraform: modules, state, drift and lifecycle100 min · Not completed

    Review reusable boundaries, migrate state safely, detect drift without applying, and understand destructive lifecycle changes.

  7. 6. AWS delivery: GitHub OIDC and reviewed Terraform plans85 min · Not completed

    Replace static CI keys with scoped federation and design a reviewed deployment boundary that untrusted pull requests cannot cross.

  8. The pieces are coming together

    Keep your notes and evidence close. The capstone is a chance to connect what you have practised, one verified step at a time.

  9. 7. AWS capstone: a secure, highly available WordPress service160 min · Not completed

    Deploy and defend two-AZ WordPress with private EC2, ALB TLS, shared EFS, Multi-AZ RDS and role-based secrets; prove failure recovery and tear it down.
